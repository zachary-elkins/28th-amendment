Research project

What should Americans add to the Constitution?

The 28th Amendment Project studies what happens when Americans explore constitutional ideas from around the world and decide what, if anything, belongs in the U.S. Constitution.

Participate in the studyVisit Constitute ↗Pilot version

How the study works

Participants are invited to look beyond the familiar U.S. constitutional debate, explore provisions from other countries, and draft a proposed 28th Amendment in their own words.

01

Explore

Use Constitute to browse constitutional rights and provisions from around the world.

02

Choose

Select a provision, principle, or approach that you believe the United States should consider.

03

Draft

Write your own proposed 28th Amendment and explain why it belongs in the U.S. Constitution.

Pilot participation

The public research instrument is still being prepared. In the pilot, participants will complete a short survey, explore Constitute in a separate tab, and return to submit a proposed amendment.

No research responses are currently being collected on this page.

Research questions

The project focuses on constitutional preferences, borrowing, and the effects of exposing citizens to constitutional alternatives outside the United States.

What do people want?Which rights and reforms rise to the top, and which produce disagreement?
Where do ideas come from?Which countries and constitutions become sources of inspiration?
How do ideas travel?How does a foreign constitutional provision change when it is translated into proposed U.S. constitutional language?
Does exploration change attitudes?Does exposure to global alternatives affect views of the U.S. Constitution or openness to amendment?

Results

As the project develops, this page will report aggregate findings: the topics participants choose, the constitutional systems they draw from, and the amendments they propose.

The goal is to make the public-facing results transparent and useful without publishing raw respondent-level research data.

About the project

The 28th Amendment Project grows out of research on public constitution making and earlier constitutional drafting exercises. It asks a simple question: when Americans are given a wider menu of constitutional possibilities, what do they choose?

Related resources
