The 28th Amendment Project

What would Americans add to the U.S. Constitution if they could draw on constitutional ideas from around the world?

About·Participate·Research·Results

About the project

The 28th Amendment Project is a research initiative on public constitutional choice. Participants explore provisions from constitutions around the world, select an idea they believe the United States should consider, and draft their own proposed constitutional amendment.

The project is designed to study both what people want to add to the Constitution and where those ideas come from. Participants use Constitute, a comparative database of the world’s constitutions, as a source of possible ideas.

Participate

Participation will involve a short survey, an exploration exercise using Constitute, and a final proposal for a 28th Amendment.

  1. Complete a short set of baseline questions.
  2. Explore constitutional rights and provisions from other countries.
  3. Select one idea that you think the United States should consider.
  4. Draft the amendment in your own words and explain your reasoning.
  5. Complete a short set of follow-up questions.

Pilot status: the research instrument is still being finalized. No response data are being collected on this site yet.

Research questions

Which constitutional ideas do participants choose?Which rights and reforms are most attractive when people are given a broad comparative menu?
Which countries become sources of constitutional ideas?Do participants borrow from familiar democracies, regional peers, highly successful countries, or unexpected sources?
How are foreign provisions translated into American constitutional language?The project records both the source of inspiration and the participant’s own proposed amendment.
Does comparative exposure change attitudes toward constitutional reform?Short pre- and post-exercise measures will assess openness to amendment and views about learning from other constitutions.

Results

After the pilot launches, this page will report aggregate patterns in participant choices, including the most frequently selected topics and the countries and constitutions that most often serve as sources of inspiration.

Individual responses will not be posted here unless the research protocol explicitly allows anonymized public display.

Project status

This site is a research prototype. The initial pilot will focus on amendment choice, source provision, proposed text, participant rationale, and a short set of pre/post constitutional attitudes. Additional designs involving deliberation, group composition, and search-path behavior may follow.
